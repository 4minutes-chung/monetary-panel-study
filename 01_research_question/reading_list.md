# Reading List

Organised by theme. Read in the order that matches where you are in the analysis.

---

## 1. Inflation Targeting — What It Is and Whether It Works

| Paper | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Bernanke & Mishkin (1997) "Inflation Targeting: A New Framework for Monetary Policy?" | *Journal of Economic Perspectives* | Original framing — what IT is supposed to do and why; good starting point |
| Roger (2010) "Inflation Targeting Turns 20" | *Finance & Development*, IMF | Survey of 20 years of IT; also contains the adoption dates table used as data input |
| Ball & Sheridan (2005) "Does Inflation Targeting Matter?" | In *The Inflation-Targeting Debate* (Bernanke & Woodford eds.) | Skeptical view — improvement in IT countries is not different from non-adopters; important counterpoint |
| Lin & Ye (2009) "Does Inflation Targeting Really Make a Difference?" | *Journal of Monetary Economics* | Propensity-score matching approach; more positive result than Ball & Sheridan; good on selection-into-treatment |
| Mishkin & Schmidt-Hebbel (2007) "Does Inflation Targeting Make a Difference?" | NBER WP 12876 | Comprehensive cross-country panel study; covers both inflation level and variance |

---

## 2. Money, Inflation, and the Quantity Theory

| Paper | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| McCandless & Weber (1995) "Some Monetary Facts" | *Minneapolis Fed Quarterly Review* | Direct predecessor to the Lucas plot in Obj A; the cross-country long-run baseline |
| De Grauwe & Polan (2005) "Is Inflation Always and Everywhere a Monetary Phenomenon?" | *Scandinavian Journal of Economics* | Panel decomposition — shows the correlation is strong for high-inflation countries but weak for low-inflation ones; directly relevant to whether IT breaks the relationship |
| Sargent & Surico (2011) "Two Illustrations of the Quantity Theory of Money" | *American Economic Review* | Shows the money-inflation correlation weakens when inflation is low and central banks credibly anchor expectations — exactly the mechanism Obj B is testing |

---

## 3. Event-Study and Staggered DiD Methods

| Paper | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Callaway & Sant'Anna (2021) "Difference-in-Differences with Multiple Time Periods" | *Journal of Econometrics* | Canonical staggered DiD estimator for staggered adoption settings like IT |
| Sun & Abraham (2021) "Estimating Dynamic Treatment Effects in Event Studies with Heterogeneous Treatment Effects" | *Journal of Econometrics* | Handles heterogeneous treatment timing; read alongside Callaway & Sant'Anna |
| Borusyak, Jaravel & Spiess (2024) "Revisiting Event-Study Designs: Robust and Efficient Estimation" | *Review of Economic Studies* | Imputation-based estimator; more efficient than the two above in many settings |
| Jordà (2005) "Estimation and Inference of Impulse Responses by Local Projections" | *Journal of the American Statistical Association* | LP foundation; use for the dynamic post-adoption path in Obj B |

---

## 4. Identification and Macro Inference

| Paper | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Nakamura & Steinsson (2018) "Identification in Macroeconomics" | *Journal of Economic Perspectives* | Best non-technical treatment of why macro identification is hard; read before finalising the design |
| Stock & Yogo (2005) "Testing for Weak Instruments in Linear IV Regression" | In *Identification and Inference for Econometric Models* | Reference for the F-stat > 10 rule used in the Phase 1 gate; relevant if IV is retained as robustness |

---

## 5. Optional Depth

| Paper | Why |
|---|---|
| Abadie (2021) "Using Synthetic Controls: Feasibility, Data Requirements, and Methodological Aspects" — *JEL* | If staggered DiD results are noisy, synthetic control per country is a cleaner robustness check |
| Ramey (2016) "Macroeconomic Shocks and Their Propagation" — *Handbook of Macroeconomics* Ch. 2 | Covers LP-IV in practice; useful context for the dynamic event-study in Obj B |
